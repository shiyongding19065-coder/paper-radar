### 1. DexPort: A Hand-Held Skill Interface for Dexterous Hand Data Collection

- Authors: Amber Xie, Jiawei Fu, Xiaofeng Guo, Carmelo Sferrazza, Guanya Shi, Haozhi Qi
- Published: 2026-10-23T17:56:28Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32969v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32969v1

Collecting high-quality demonstrations remains a central bottleneck for learning dexterous manipulation. Teleoperation records executable robot actions, but it requires a complete robot setup and places the burden of coordinating many finger joints on the operator. Human demonstrations are easier to collect, but their motions must be estimated and retargeted to the robot, and they do not directly provide robot actions. In this paper, we introduce DexPort, a portable data-collection interface for high-DoF robot hands. It uses the same hand hardware for collection and deployment, avoiding a hand embodiment gap, and records both commanded joint targets and measured joint states. Our key observation is that a small set of skills can support a wide range of dexterous tasks. During collection, the operator moves the hand-held interface to specify the robot wrist motion and selects or adjusts skills that coordinate the finger motion. This removes the need to control each joint independently while retaining behaviors such as precise grasping and in-hand manipulation. We collect demonstrations across several real-world tasks and in unstructured environments without a robot arm. We then use behavior cloning to train visuomotor policies directly on the recorded wrist motions and hand commands, without skill labels. We evaluate across 4 tasks: pick-and-place with diverse objects, screwing with in-hand tool use, drawer stowage which requires simultaneous grasp and pulling, and a bimanual scanning task. We collect over 2,500 demonstrations with our portable device, in both laboratory and in-the-wild settings. Across our tasks, we see an improvement from 11% to 64% when using recorded robot commands instead of states, and an improvement from 36% to 78% for task success when using in-the-wild data.

### 2. J-Comm: Jacobian-Guided Latent Communication between LLM Agents

- Authors: Xiangwei Wang, Wei Wang, Sachith Seneviratne, Ken Chen, Yu Xia, Peng Wang, Saman Halgamuge
- Published: 2026-10-23T16:42:38Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32871v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32871v1

Latent communication requires both an effective continuous reasoning process and an interface through which an external model can leverage its intermediate computation. We present J-Comm, a framework comprising two core components. First, a training-free Geometric Agreement for Latent Reasoning (GEAR) reweights token candidates over existing model embeddings, which monotonically optimizes geometric consensus among candidate tokens in embedding space at each step. Second, we introduce an H+J (Hidden-and-Jacobian) communication interface that gives the Receiver two complementary views of the Sender's reasoning. The H channel supplies continuous inputs derived from feedback distributions, while the J channel conveys intermediate-state cues in vocabulary-indexed Jacobian-lens coordinates. Intuitively, H provides inputs for the Receiver to reason from, while J offers additional guidance within its computation. The Receiver reconstructs these cues using its own dictionary and injects them into intermediate layers during prefill, enabling communication without sharing key-value caches or requiring identical model widths. In Qwen3-8B to Qwen3-4B communication, J-Comm achieves the highest mean score across seven benchmarks, including 94.80% on MATH500, 54.55% on GPQA-Diamond, 73.33% on AIME2024+2025, and 62.93% success on ALFWorld. Compared with TextMAS, it improves performance by 1.29-8.59 percentage points and reduces generated text tokens by 19.2-74.8%.

### 3. Reimagining Enterprise RAG for Multi-Agent Systems: A Knowledge Layer That Organizes Before It Retrieves

- Authors: Garima Agrawal, Prasun Das, Akshay Sarvade, Priyanka L, Minisha N, Sravani Joshna, Hemath Manivannan, Lavanya Korada et al.
- Published: 2026-10-23T16:21:56Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32851v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32851v1

Enterprises are deploying agentic AI across the software development lifecycle, and they need the behavioral logic of the systems they act on, not stakeholder documentation. A seven-agent reverse-engineering pipeline recovered this logic, processing 241 repositories across 18 services into thousands of artifacts describing scope, journeys, rules and acceptance criteria. Recovery does not confer usability. Entities like offer, eligibility, and activation recur across services under a shared vocabulary, related in name but operationally distinct, so one end-to-end journey implicates over a thousand near-identical rules that must not be conflated. Flat vector retrieval fails, returning an identical rule from the wrong service at high similarity with no signal of error. Content-level ontology, the traditional remedy, did not exist, and relating every occurrence of recurring entities would have cost more than the engagement and not survived a release cycle. We place the ontology at the artifact level. In this machine-generated corpus, each artifact retains its producer, component, service, domain and type, provenance that disambiguates before content is read. We present a knowledge layer that organizes before retrieving: a self-describing catalog, a sparse metadata registry, and a navigation hypergraph defined over artifacts rather than extracted entities. Queries resolve to artifact identifiers that constrain vector search, so the competing rule from another service is absent rather than outranked, making disambiguation structural, not statistical. Built in under a week and admitting new services by convention, the layer serves SDLC agents, developers, quality engineers and analysts. It supported root-cause analysis and resolution of 100+ production defects with developer verification and attributed 35 release regression failures to specific rules and services.

### 4. AFRA: Age-Threshold Frame-Based Random Access for Timely Communication in Multi-Agent Systems

- Authors: Jiedan Tan, Fang Liu
- Published: 2026-10-23T15:32:43Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32785v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32785v1

Timely state updates are essential for coordination and monitoring in multi-agent systems, such as teams of mobile robots exchanging state information. When agents share a wireless channel, collisions and slow convergence of distributed access schemes can degrade information freshness over finite operating periods. This paper proposes age-threshold frame-based random access (AFRA), a distributed medium access control protocol for minimizing age of information (AoI). Once its age reaches a threshold, an agent starts a local frame with a mandatory transmission and, if unsuccessful, makes one further attempt at a uniformly selected remaining slot. Each attempt carries a freshly generated update. AFRA requires only local state and acknowledgment feedback, with shared slot boundaries but independently timed local frames. We prove almost-sure convergence from any legal initial state to collision-free periodic operation when the frame length $L$ and age threshold $T$ satisfy $L\ge T>N\ge2$, where $N$ is the number of agents. We also derive closed-form expressions for steady-state throughput, average AoI, and average peak AoI. Simulations demonstrate that AFRA combines rapid convergence at small age thresholds with lower window-averaged AoI than the existing age-aware random access schemes evaluated in this study.

### 5. Density control of large-scale multi-agent systems via tuning of agent interactions

- Authors: Mena Zkhary, Alain Boldini
- Published: 2026-10-23T15:26:49Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32778v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32778v1

Collective behavior in biological systems emerges through adaptive interactions among units, enabling large populations to coordinate without centralized control. This mechanism raises the question of whether collective behavior in engineered multi-agent systems can likewise be regulated by modifying the interaction law between agents, rather than by directly controlling individual units. To answer this question, we consider large-scale multi-agent systems evolving on a ring and treat the interaction kernel as a time-varying control variable whose evolution regulates the collective dynamics. Using a mean-field formulation, we develop a framework for the analysis and design of kernel-based controllers. We derive explicit conditions for controllability in application-relevant scenarios, and synthesize a state-feedback controller to regulate the kernel dynamics, validated on the microscopic, discrete dynamics. Numerical simulations demonstrate the approach's effectiveness and robustness to unmodeled perturbations. This work constitutes a first step in the design of bio-inspired control strategies for large-scale multi-agent systems by modulation of interactions between agents.

### 6. ActionRAG: Evidence-Refining Graph Diffusion for Long-Term Memory in LLM Agents

- Authors: Navyansh Mahla, Sashank Vandrangi
- Published: 2026-10-23T14:59:37Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32752v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32752v1

LLM agents need long-term memory that can recover precise and mutually consistent evidence from growing interaction histories, particularly when answers depend on relations among facts rather than isolated semantic matches. Current memory pipelines remain vulnerable at two stages: indexing errors can fragment repeated entities or merge distinct ones, and online retrieval often treats initial similarity scores as final even when the surrounding graph provides evidence for revising them. We introduce ActionRAG, a graph memory architecture that couples cautious indexing with query-time graph retroevaluation. Its indexing pipeline reconciles type labels and applies tiered entity resolution. Given a query, dual-channel entity and triple retrieval forms an initial evidence set and induces a bounded local graph. Query-conditioned receiver-normalized diffusion refines triple scores using local path structure. Seed protection, hub-penalized flux, and triple materialization preserve direct evidence while recovering missed connections. We evaluate ActionRAG on MemoryAgentBench (MAB), which tests accurate retrieval, conflict resolution, long-range understanding, and test-time learning. Across its eight deterministic-scoring columns, ActionRAG achieves 55.1 average accuracy, improving over the strongest retrieval-based memory baseline by 5.4 points. Under MAB's query-time cost accounting, mean synthesis cost stays below \$0.001 per query on the four reported cost columns, and the sum of separately measured retrieval-side p99 components is 440.7 ms on our consumer Apple Silicon setup.

### 7. OpenDraco: A Platform for Engineering and Experimenting with Configurable Multi-Agent Architectures for Software Engineering

- Authors: Xiang Feng Ye Pan, Vincenzo De Martino, Matias Martinez
- Published: 2026-10-23T14:17:18Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32686v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32686v1

Experimenting with LLM-based multi-agent systems (MAS) involves an iterative cycle of modeling configurations, executing and evaluating them, comparing results, and refining system designs. Existing approaches provide limited end-to-end support for this experimentation lifecycle. We present OpenDraco, an open-source platform that integrates these activities into a single workflow. OpenDraco represents agents, prompts, tools, models, parameters, and communication topology in exportable, versioned JSON specifications, translated into executable LangGraph workflows supporting different interaction structures. Users can modify and re-evaluate configurations using local or cloud-based LLMs. We instantiate OpenDraco for automated program repair (APR) and evaluate a sequential baseline and seven reconstructed configurations on synthetic and real-world SWE-bench Lite issues. The results reveal substantial cost differences among configurations with similar repair outcomes, while the baseline resolves 12 of 77 SWE-bench issues using open-source LLMs. Additional proof-of-concept tasks illustrate applicability beyond APR. OpenDraco supports iterative experimentation with heterogeneous MAS configurations, enabling researchers to examine their outcomes and costs within a common environment. ### 8. StateMirror: Pre-Execution Detection of Hallucinated Tool Calls in Coding Agents

- Authors: Shiwen Shan, Yintong Huo, Zhiying Wu, Yuxin Su
- Published: 2026-10-23T13:56:13Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32648v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32648v1

Large language model (LLM)-based coding agents may propose invalid actions that are not applicable in the execution environment, such as accessing non-existent paths or missing packages. We term this mismatch hallucinated tool use. A preliminary study of 4,398 agent trajectories reveals that these hallucinations are pervasive and costly, requiring a median of 3 additional interaction steps and 22.4K tokens to recover. Existing frameworks rely on reactive, post-execution feedback, wasting runtime resources. Nevertheless, since an agent's execution environment is grounded in concrete codebase and harness components, many action prerequisites can be extracted and validated prior to execution. To this end, we introduce StateMirror, a lightweight pre-execution detector. StateMirror intercepts actions at the harness boundary, extracts state predicates via AST and command analysis, and deterministically validates them against the live repository. By pinpointing exact environment mismatches before execution, StateMirror preempts invalid actions and subsequent recovery cycles. Evaluation on 2,508 actions shows StateMirror achieves 0.940 precision and 0.817 F1 in detecting such hallucinations, surpassing the strongest LLM judge by 35.3% in F1 while reducing median detection latency from 17.73 s to 1.50 ms with zero token consumption. For practical mitigation, StateMirror's pre-execution feedback enables agents to rectify over 95% of hallucinated tool calls. During live mitigation on 79 tasks, StateMirror reduces agent execution time by 15.8% and token consumption by 11.8% while maintaining task resolution.

### 9. What Transfers Across Protein Pockets? Experience Reuse in Molecular Optimization for Agentic Structure-Based Drug Design

- Authors: Zaifei Yang, Weiyu Chen, Yaqing Wang, James Kwok
- Published: 2026-10-23T13:38:42Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32620v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32620v1

LLM agents optimize a ligand in a protein pocket by editing it step by step and docking each edit. Recent agents first spend extra dockings probing the complex with controlled edits; guidance induced from these probes is reported to help more than the chemical knowledge the model already carries. The probes are then discarded with the instance that produced them, so the obvious next step is to keep them in an experience base, letting a new pocket inherit what earlier ones already paid for. Such a base is read through a \emph{key}: the record properties under which past edits are pooled and a new one is looked up. Using the full log of one such run over the CrossDocked2020 test-set pockets, we ask whether its keys recur on a pocket the base has never seen and still separate improving from worsening edits. \crchange{Using} the contacts at the site being edited, the action, and the round as \crchange{a} key, the base separates them sharply. Within our analysis, this separation is largely explained by how much the edit changed the molecule's size, the action, and the round, none of which describes the target. That is not because the target is irrelevant: naming it outright separates them several times more sharply. For a pocket absent from the base, transfer must rely on a description rather than its identity. Among the exact-match descriptions we evaluate, the more specific ones rarely recur, whereas the coarser ones provide little separation; the signals that do transfer primarily describe the edit and the molecule it produced rather than the target. Under the representations and lookup scheme studied here, an experience base may be more useful for prioritizing an agent's own proposals for docking than for directly prescribing what to build.

### 10. Tool-Calling LLM Agents Under Realistic MCP Faults: Scale Does Not Purchase Fault Tolerance

- Authors: Antonio Ken Iannillo, Joshua Segun Owotogbe, Roberto Natella, Francesco Avallone, Kristina Kudryavtseva, Indika Kumara
- Published: 2026-10-23T12:35:51Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32555v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32555v1

LLM agents increasingly rely on external tools exposed via the Model Context Protocol (MCP), yet current benchmarks assume tools respond correctly and on time, leaving agent resilience to realistic tool failures untested. We introduce MCP-Injector, a drop-in, protocol-aware fault-injection middleware that interposes on the MCP tool-call path with controlled persistence (permanent, transient, intermittent) and seedable schedules. Its fault model is calibrated to the observed incident distribution (failure mode x persistence) of the public MCP ecosystem, mined from 57,240 issue and pull-request threads across 1,774 open-source MCP repositories. In a 1,350-cell experiment crossing two backbone sizes (20B, 120B), three agent stacks (custom multi-round plan-and-execute, OpenAI Agents SDK, LangChain), and three fault injection conditions, we find that faults drawn from this permanent-heavy stress profile cause severe quality degradation (task-completion score falls by 60%, from 8.08 to 3.23 out of 10), and that scale does not purchase fault tolerance for the two gpt-oss backbones we test: the 120B retains no larger share of its fault-free score under faults (resilience-ratio model, $p = 0.96$), and its completion rate under faults is, if anything, lower (91.1% vs. 95.6%; difference 4.4 points, 95% CI $[-0.2, 9.3]$). Agent stacks differ in how well they absorb faults, which suggests that framework-level error handling matters more than model scale. These results demonstrate that capability benchmarks alone can mask operational brittleness, motivating protocol-aware fault injection as a complementary evaluation dimension for tool-calling agents.
