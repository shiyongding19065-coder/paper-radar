### 1. Provable Coordination for LLM Agents via Message Sequence Charts

- Authors: Benedikt Bollig, Matthias Függer, Thomas Nowak
- Published: 2026-10-17
- Source: openalex
- DOI: 10.1007/978-3-032-40121-2_12
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-40121-2_12
- PDF: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-032-40121-2_12.pdf

Abstract Multi-agent systems built on large language models (LLMs) are difficult to reason about. Coordination errors such as deadlocks or type-mismatched messages are often hard to detect through testing. We introduce a domain-specific language for specifying agent coordination based on message sequence charts (MSCs). The language separates message-passing structure from LLM calls, tool calls, and human control points, whose outcomes remain unpredictable. We define the syntax and semantics of the language and present a syntax-directed projection that generates deadlock-free local agent programs from global coordination specifications. We illustrate the approach with a diagnosis consensus protocol and show how coordination properties can be established independently of LLM nondeterminism. We also describe a runtime planning extension in which an LLM dynamically generates a coordination workflow for which the same structural guarantees apply. An open-source Python implementation of our framework is available as ZipperGen.

### 2. AI-Driven Reproducibility and Analytical Robustness in Scientific Discovery

- Authors: Shicheng Liu, Krista Opsahl-Ong, Andrew Drozdov, Julian Killingback, Arnav Singhvi, Ronak Pradeep, Michael Bendersky, James C. Dickerson et al.
- Published: 2026-10-16T17:57:55Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23845v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23845v1

Reproducible and analytically robust research findings are essential to scientific progress, but evaluating these properties typically requires costly manual effort. We investigate whether LLM agents can help automate reproduction and robustness analysis, using a medical case study grounded in the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database, a widely used U.S. cancer registry underlying tens of thousands of published studies. First, we introduce SEER-Repro, a benchmark of 20 published SEER-based papers together with the data and resources required to reproduce their findings. Second, we create an AI agent that successfully reproduces 77.4% of 2,190 atomic claims, while providing executable baselines for further analysis. Third, we develop an agentic analysis pipeline to test the robustness of the medical findings by identifying analytical choices underlying each finding, proposing defensible alternatives, and rerunning the analysis to determine whether those choices affect the conclusion. Across published papers in SEER-Repro, our pipeline identifies potentially consequential analytical choices in 9.5% of fully reproduced findings and flags them for expert review. These results show how LLM agents can support scientific review by reproducing findings, systematically testing their robustness, and directing expert attention toward potentially consequential analytical choices.

### 3. Jarvis: Coordination Deserves a Brain of Its Own in Agentic Interactive Models

- Authors: Yuxiang Wang, Shengbo Cai, Zhixian Zhao, Yingda Shen, Yuancheng Wang, Xueyao Zhang, Liqiang Zhang, Teddy Sun et al.
- Published: 2026-10-16T17:29:10Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23804v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23804v1

Interactive models increasingly hand complex reasoning and tool use to a slow brain in the background, leaving a realtime front model free to sustain full-duplex dialogue. Yet the user may refine, cancel or replace a request while its task runs, so a plan or result that made sense a moment ago goes stale. Each new utterance must therefore be read against the work in flight, but the front model follows only the conversation and the back end sees only its own task. Coordination deserves a brain of its own, one that sees both. We introduce Jarvis, a coordination harness where models judge and code enforces. Its typed decision model, JEV, reads each turn against the live tasks and picks among operations built from a versioned task record. Code checks every choice against the current state before applying it, so no decision can act on a task version the user has replaced. Jarvis also rechecks plans before they execute and results before they are spoken, and counts an answer as delivered only once the user has heard it in full. We build two instances of this design. Jarvis-Audio runs unchanged on three full-duplex speech models. Jarvis-Omni adds vision and rechecks the evidence behind an answer before and during playback, since a scene can change while nobody speaks. With the front model and back end held fixed, replacing Qwen Audio Agent with Jarvis raises tau-Voice task success more than sixfold, from 29 to 192 of 278 tasks, and the gains hold on every benchmark and front model. Against published systems, Jarvis-Audio reaches 69% Pass1 on Full-Duplex-Bench v3 with an open 9B front model, versus 60% for GPT-Realtime. These results suggest that how the two brains are coordinated matters as much as how strong they are.

### 4. On the possibilities and limitations of LLM-based multi-agent systems for multi-criteria decision-making in healthcare operations management

- Authors: Marco Boresta, Tommaso Giovannelli, Sepehr Namirad, Giulia Riccardi
- Published: 2026-10-16T16:05:15Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23673v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23673v1

Multi-criteria models for healthcare operations need weights that express how stakeholders trade off patient waiting time, provider workload, and operating cost, but these weights are difficult to quantify. Teams of large language model (LLM) agents, each prompted to represent a stakeholder, have been used to supply them. We propose a framework for assessing the reliability of LLM-generated preferences as inputs to multi-objective optimization and quantifying how their uncertainty affects decisions. We study explicit preferences by asking agents to rate the importance of the criteria in an outpatient appointment scheduling problem, and implicit preferences by inferring trade-offs from the agents' pairwise choices between emergency department configurations. The computational experiments show that the explicit preferences respond to the clinic's operating conditions and differ across roles, but deliberation among the agents can erase these differences, whereas simple averaging preserves them. The pairwise choices used to reveal the agents' implicit preferences depend largely on which option is presented first, and we find limited evidence that a statistical model using contextual information alongside presentation order predicts the agents' choices on new comparison pairs better than a model using presentation order alone. We then formulate robust weighted-sum optimization models to account for preference uncertainty. In most cases, these models select different schedules or configurations from those obtained using average preference weights. These results indicate that LLM-generated preferences need an audit of this kind before they are used as optimization weights in healthcare decision support.

### 5. ABF-Bench: A Verifiable Benchmark for Agentic Blockchain Forensics

- Authors: Xiaohang Yu, Hejia Geng, William Knottenbelt
- Published: 2026-10-16T15:51:03Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23656v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23656v1

Blockchain forensics is adversarial and open-ended: money launderers split, swap, and bridge funds, cut their trail at mixers or custodial services, and change tactics from one incident to the next. Investigators must keep adapting their tracing in response. Yet the immutability of public ledgers preserves every recorded movement in public view, making much of the investigation independently verifiable. We introduce ABF-Bench, an interactive benchmark that makes evidence-backed fund tracing a verifiable agent task, in which every claimed movement must cite ledger records the agent retrieved. A case-to-task pipeline builds well-posed tasks across ledgers: conservative rules halt tracing wherever the records leave a continuation unresolved, and each task's reference graph is the answer those rules determine. Built from real laundering activity, the tasks combine multi-hop transfers, DeFi exchanges, cross-chain round trips, custodial and mixer deposits, and address poisoning. We also propose the ABF agent harness, built around a working memory, and evaluate it alongside general-purpose agents and fixed-order tracers. The harness reaches 75% edge recall, against 47% for Codex with the same model and 10% for Claude Code, which loses recall to overconfidence: it cites records it left unretrieved. Compared with fixed-order tracers, the harness acquires evidence faster than the best of them early in the budget and recognizes undisclosed custodial services that they walk past (89% vs. 0%), with 98% fidelity. However, agents tend to stop early and lose recall on larger tasks, which holds their final recall below the best tracer. Beyond exposing these gaps, ABF-Bench opens blockchain forensics to LLM agents as a verifiable domain, where the verdicts that score agents also offer potential training signals.

### 6. From Trajectories to Differentiated Skills: Cognitive Differentiation for Self-Improving Agents

- Authors: Yewei Zhao, Derong Xu, Wei Chen, Yin Xu, Peilun Zhou, Xiaohan Wang, Guojun Yin, Wei Lin et al.
- Published: 2026-10-16T15:16:56Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23599v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23599v1

Large language model (LLM) agents tackle complex tasks through reasoning and interaction with external environments. Agent skills provide persistent, transferable capabilities that guide task planning, inform contextual decisions, support tool use, and enable executable procedures. Yet existing skill-evolution methods largely organize knowledge around skill artifacts, overlooking the functional heterogeneity within individual skills and its implications for knowledge maintenance and use. Inspired by cognitive science, we propose Cognitive Differentiation in Skill Evolution (CDSE), which evolves a unified skill through four functionally distinct yet coordinated components: Meta, Experience, Tool, and Script. CDSE extracts evidence-grounded semantic gradients from successful and failed trajectories, consolidates them by functional role, and applies component-specific updates and validation with cross-component coordination and adaptive invocation. An analysis of 500 top-ranked skill packages shows that 96.8% contain knowledge spanning at least two functional roles. Across six benchmarks, three agent models, and two execution harnesses, CDSE achieves the highest scores in all 36 settings, improving over the Base agent by 7.6 percentage points and the strongest competing method in each setting by 2.6 points on average. Ablation studies further support the importance of functional differentiation and adaptive invocation.

### 7. RL-Guided Temporal Localization for Dual-Channel Retrieval in Long-Horizon Agent Memory

- Authors: Dabin Sheng, Zhe Wu, Jinming Zhao, Junliang Xing, Yuanchun Shi
- Published: 2026-10-16T15:16:50Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23598v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23598v1

Time-aware retrieval is crucial for long-horizon LLM agents to effectively leverage ever-growing long-term memory. Existing methods focus on lexical or semantic similarity, leading to target memories being crowded out by temporally mismatched yet semantically similar candidates. Even time-aware approaches inject temporal cues only during memory organization or answer generation, overlooking temporal constraints at the retrieval stage, where candidate selection occurs - the root cause of the displacement. Inspired by the temporal contiguity effect in human episodic memory, we propose TIDER (Temporal Interval-driven Dual-channel Evidence Retrieval), a time-aware agentic retrieval framework. Central to TIDER is a reinforcement learning (RL)-trained temporal interval locator that infers the temporal windows in which the evidence lies. RL optimizes window localization as a discrete, non-differentiable retrieval decision, using composite rewards to convert sparse answer supervision into fine-grained retrieval feedback. These temporal windows first define the temporal retrieval channel; the retrieved candidates are then merged with results from the semantic channel and reranked by proximity to the windows. This temporal grounding enables TIDER to retrieve temporally aligned evidence that would otherwise be drowned out by the volume of semantically similar yet temporally mismatched memories. With a lightweight 1.7B locator, TIDER achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on both long-horizon memory benchmarks - 61.52% on LifeBench (+8.82%) and 78.96% on LoCoMo (+3.66%) - which indicates that temporal cues are crucial for pinpointing target evidence amid a vast pool of semantically similar historical events in long-term daily-life memory.

### 8. SkillBrowser: Selective Skill Exposure for Scalable Skill-Using Agents

- Authors: Jin Ye, Yifan Jiang, Lan-zhe Guo
- Published: 2026-10-16T14:44:01Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23554v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23554v1

Large language model agents increasingly rely on skills to extend their capabilities in complex tasks. However, scalable skill use requires Selective Skill Exposure (SSE): providing relevant procedural content when needed while avoiding unnecessary exposure that may disrupt reasoning. Existing paradigms fall short in complementary ways. Progressive disclosure exposes metadata for a growing skill library, introducing context pollution; retrieval limits catalog exposure, but static retrieval from the initial task query may be misaligned with capability needs that emerge during reasoning and may still inject unnecessary skills. We propose SkillBrowser, a Meta-Skill-managed framework that enables an agent to identify current capability needs, formulate targeted queries, and selectively load skills on demand. Across six SRA-Bench subsets and five LLMs, SkillBrowser improves aggregate accuracy over static retrieval and LLM-based selection, including multi-step tool use, and mitigates accuracy degradation on already-solvable instances. Benefits remain model- and task-dependent, with under-invocation of useful skills and additional latency as key limitations. These results demonstrate the viability of Meta-Skill-managed SSE for scalable skill repositories.

### 9. Robust Function Calling for LLM Agents Under Interface Drift

- Authors: Aniruddha Chattopadhyay, Kanishka Halder, Partha Sarathi Roy
- Published: 2026-10-16T11:49:37Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23342v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23342v1

Tool-augmented language-model agents rely on interface text: tool descriptions, argument schemas, and the candidate tool list shown at inference time. These interfaces often change after deployment even when the underlying tool semantics remain fixed, creating a practical reliability problem for function-calling systems. We study this failure mode as interface drift and evaluate whether a lightweight, training-free repair layer can recover robustness without retraining the base model. Our method, SchemaShield-Lite, combines schema validation, candidate-set retry for missing tool names, and one-shot constrained repair. On Qwen3.5-9B, it raises DICE-300 accuracy from 0.717 to 0.813 and BFCL-200 accuracy from 0.580 to 0.805, recovering 19/22 and 33/45 drift-induced failures with no observed repair harms. Naive retry does not explain the gains. Ablations show that candidate-set drift, especially distractor tools, accounts for most observed damage, while description and schema edits alone are less harmful. The results position validation-and-repair as a practical robustness layer for deployed LLM-agent tool use.

### 10. Wait for It: A Component-Level Analysis of Latency Feedback Mechanisms for LLM Agents in VR

- Authors: Pratham Arora, Abhinav Lodha, Siddharth Siddharth
- Published: 2026-10-16T11:40:21Z
- Source: arxiv
- DOI: None
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23336v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23336v1

Delays in AI responses pose a significant challenge to immersive Virtual Reality experiences. While previous research demonstrates that feedback can reduce the perception of waiting, we lack understanding of which types work best and why. We conducted a within-subjects study with 18 participants to systematically compare three isolated feedback approaches during VR agent interactions: verbal fillers (e.g., um..., hmm...), visual indicators (loading spinners), and embodied gestures (thinking animations). Our results revealed that embodied gestures significantly outperformed visual loading indicators across multiple dimensions, making agents feel more lifelike and socially present. Interestingly, explicit visual feedback like loading spinners actually degraded perceived humanness and broke immersion by forcing users to shift from social to technical mental models. These findings suggest that natural, behavior-based feedback maintains the illusion of conversing with a thinking entity, while technical indicators expose the computational substrate. We present empirically-grounded design guidelines for creating more engaging AI-powered VR agents.
