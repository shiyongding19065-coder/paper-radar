### 1. Beyond the Agent Boundary

- Authors: Pasquale Kaboth
- Published: 2026-09-18
- Source: openalex
- DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22837961
- Link: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22837961
- PDF: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22837961

Beyond the Agent Boundary investigates whether the nominal software boundaries of individual AI agents are sufficient for describing the causal and safety-relevant boundaries of recurrent multi-agent systems. The paper develops the hypothesis that persistent shared memory, recurrent communication, adaptive coupling, and authority-bearing state transitions can create system-level causal dependencies that extend beyond individual agents. Under sufficiently persistent recurrent coupling, nominally separate agents may exhibit what the paper terms meta-organism-like collective dynamics: measurable population-level patterns of perturbation propagation, shared-state persistence, distributed damping or amplification, and control. The term is used strictly as an operational systems concept and does not imply biological organismhood, phenomenal consciousness, or the existence of a unified subject. To investigate these dynamics, the study introduces the Causal Boundary Stress Test (CBST), an intervention- and ablation-based framework that separates causal reach, persistence, amplification, propagation time, authority crossing, and provenance integrity. A reproducible surrogate-agent experimental program examines population scaling, ring, sparse and dense coupling topologies, persistent shared memory, memory persistence, communication and memory-read delays, adversarial memory writing, and explicit authority gating. The extended campaign comprises 1,980 simulation runs, including population sizes from 25 to 200 agents, topology comparisons, coupling-strength sweeps, memory-persistence sweeps, and adversarial shared-state experiments. The results demonstrate within the tested surrogate model that population size alone is not a sufficient descriptor of causal integration. Network topology and shared persistent state can substantially alter the causal footprint of a local intervention. Shared memory can operate as a population-wide causal bus, while global causal reach can occur without large or persistent population-level deviation. Adversarial memory-writing experiments further show that separating information influence from authoritative state mutation can strongly reduce unauthorized amplification without eliminating ordinary communication. A release-audit experiment additionally corrects an earlier working-draft delay condition by explicitly delaying the shared-memory read path. Across the tested delays, eventual causal reach remains population-wide while propagation time increases, supporting a channel-specific distinction between causal reach and causal latency within the declared surrogate model. The paper relates these findings to the PFCA trajectory-native Field-Core architecture, particularly its separation of observation, proposal, validation, canonical commit, external effect, identity, lineage, and provenance. It argues that communication and causal influence should not automatically imply memory-write authority, action authority, canonical state authority, or identity fusion. The work also incorporates and explicitly attributes provenance-related constraints developed through the September 2026 D12RG collaboration with Luke Kenneth Casson Leighton, including provenance-scoped capabilities, explicit preservation bridges between independent lineages or representations, and adversarial protection against manufacturing joint evidential authority through naive lineage merging. The present evidence is theoretical and simulation-based. The experiments use controlled nonlinear surrogate agents rather than production LLM agents. Consequently, the reported numerical values are properties of the specified experimental model and are not presented as empirical constants for deployed multi-agent AI systems. A preregistered replication using real LLM agents, persistent memory systems, tools, authority boundaries, and independently logged intervention/control trajectories is identified as the next external-validation stage.
